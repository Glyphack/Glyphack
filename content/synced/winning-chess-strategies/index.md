I got interested into chess after a friend sent me some videos of a fun chess commentary.
Before that the only time I played was with my dad when I was 9 or something.

I started learning chess from watching videos and playing. Mostly watching GothamChess, and Eric Rosen, and [Yasser Seriawan](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLX4FVTozcLTfKzNuqwoptJI623wvSuFsb).

Around 6 months after watching and playing I saw little improvements in my game, and wanted to read a book.
This is the book I chose because I liked the teaching style of Yasser more than the others that I knew.

It was a fantastic book.
I read it in about a month with a chess board to recreate the games that are in the book.
In each section the book focuses on one topic or strategy.
Then once you can spot a strategy in a position you cannot unsee it. 
It's like when you see a face in the clouds then they don't look like clouds anymore.

After this book I also studied a few openings. Mostly from the FCO book.
I knew Sicilian defense, French Defense, London System.
My Elo rating reached 1400 and at some point I played a couple of matches with 1600 and 1700 opponents and won(This was during a physical education class that I chose Chess for the sport I want to play), so reading the book paid off really well.
